Fringe Ruler

Move a fine screen a little. Watch a broad band move a lot — in the opposite direction.

Fine high-contrast stripes can be uncomfortable. Nothing animates automatically; stop if viewing causes discomfort. Not a measuring instrument or navigation aid.

Two masks. One oversized motion.

The top rows show the separate masks; the bottom row overlaps them. Distances below are model pixels, not physical screen pixels.

The catch: sensitivity is not certainty.

Can a still picture tell you how far the mask moved?

Positions 0, 26 and 52 give exactly the same pattern. From this picture alone you cannot tell which one you are seeing. The button adds 26; past the slider limit it resets to 0.

The readout knows the movement because you supplied it with a slider. The image alone does not. Magnifying a small change does not remove periodic ambiguity.

What is actually moving?

The masks have pitches of 24 and 26 model pixels. Dark bands are 45% of each pitch. The broad alignment envelope repeats every 312 model pixels. Moving the rear mask right by 1 moves this envelope left by 12. The dark stripes themselves have not grown.

This is geometric overlap, not a simulation of light-wave diffraction. The display uses pixel sampling; browser scaling can add artifacts. The envelope position is an analytic phase marker, not a detected edge of a particular dark band.

x = (k − d/26) / (1/24 − 1/26), where d is rear-mask displacement and k is an integer. Follow one k continuously to get the opposite-direction motion.
